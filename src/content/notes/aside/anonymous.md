---
title: Anonymous hosting
description: Hosts whose own pages sell sign-up without identification, kept beside the register rather than in it.
lead: Sign up with an email address, pay in cryptocurrency, and the host does not know who you are.
---

These hosts advertise anonymity as the product: an account needs nothing but an email address, and payment in cryptocurrency keeps even that loose. The same feature serves opposite ends. A journalist protecting a source, a whistleblower platform, an activist or an opposition campaign under a hostile government, a researcher publishing what a company would rather keep offline: for them, a host that cannot hand over a name is the point. The same shield draws people who have a reason to hide from the law rather than from a state that abuses it: pirated software and film, phishing pages, scam shops, malware and the servers that control it. Whatever their terms ban, none of them knows who runs the server next to yours. That is why they sit beside the register rather than in it. The test is the provider's own wording, quoted on each record: a host that asks for no identification without selling the fact stays in the register. Where the servers stand matters here more than anywhere, so read the [regions](/regions/) of each record as a legal question.

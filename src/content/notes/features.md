description: What comes with the hosting beyond running the code — collaboration, staging, domains, DNS, email, a CDN, a test URL — and why none of them is a record of its own.
---

A hosting plan is rarely the whole job. Some of this is a second invoice — domains have to be registered somewhere, DNS served by someone, email run on a machine that is not the one serving the site, and most application platforms include none of the three while a shared host often includes all of them. The rest is whether the platform expects more than one person: **collaboration** is recorded because a platform where the account *is* the login is a different product from one where a team, or a client, has a place of their own, and no pricing page says which it is until somebody needs it. **Staging** is the same kind of fact — for a good many providers it is what the next tier up is for. Each is recorded as included, sold as an add-on, or not offered, because "they will sell it separately" is the ordinary answer and a yes-or-no has nowhere to put it. A record showing nothing here is one nobody has checked, not one with seven noes. Each of these is also a business somebody runs on its own, which the register carries as a category rather than a feature — [registrars](/categories/registrar/) most of all, since the company selling the name is very often the one selling the hosting too. The two answer different questions: the category says who sells the thing, this says whether it arrives with the hosting. [Managed databases](/databases/) get a facet of their own on top, because there the answer worth filtering on is which engine rather than whether.

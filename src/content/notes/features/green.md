---
title: Hosting providers that publish a green energy claim
description: Which hosting providers say something checkable about the energy they run on, what the three kinds of green claim actually mean, and why absent means unknown.
---

**Nearly every hosting provider says it is green. The claims are not the same claim**, and the difference is the part marketing depends on nobody drawing. In rising order of what they mean: **offsets** are bought after the fact, from somebody else, and are the cheapest thing to say. **Annual matching** buys as many renewable certificates over a year as the company consumed, which is what most "100% renewable" marketing means. **24/7 carbon-free** matches consumption hour by hour on the same grid, and only a handful of operators can claim it.

Which of the three a provider claims is on its record, under Other features, along with the page the claim was read on. This list is the coarser question: has this provider published anything at all? A host that names its claim and links the evidence has done something a host that says nothing has not. That is all a place on this list means — the claim is the provider's, not ours, and nothing here verifies it.

The [Green Web Foundation](https://www.thegreenwebfoundation.org/) does verify, against published evidence, and reverifies annually. Where a provider is in their directory its record carries the id and links there, rather than restating their conclusion as ours.

Their check has a limit worth knowing, because it is the reason a green host can look ungreen. It resolves by IP address, and a provider is only ever as visible as its upstream: [AWS was archived from their dataset in March 2025](https://www.thegreenwebfoundation.org/news/an-update-on-finding-representatives-for-large-hosting-providers/) for want of anyone inside Amazon to own the listing, so every site hosted on it now answers "not green" whatever its own verification says. Several providers on this list are in exactly that position.

Absent means unknown, never bad. Most records here say nothing about energy, and silence is not a finding — a four-person host that has never written a sustainability page may well run cleaner than a hyperscaler with a glossy report. Nor does any of this measure the emissions of the thing you would actually deploy: it describes a company's published position, which is a different subject from your application's footprint.

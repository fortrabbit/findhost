description: How long a hosting bill keeps arriving after the decision to leave — notice periods and minimum terms, from a day to over a year.
---

The clock measures **committed spend**: money still owed for time that will not be used. It is not the billing period, and it is not when the last invoice lands. A provider that meters usage, bills in arrears and holds nobody to a term is **within a day** — stop the service today and nothing further accrues, even though an invoice for the days already run arrives at the end of the month. What lands there is payment for time that was used, which is not a commitment.

Three mechanisms produce the longer bands, and they are worth telling apart. A **minimum term** obliges a customer to a period whether or not the service is used. A **notice period** starts a clock on the day of the decision. **Money paid in advance** — an annual plan, a prepaid year, a discounted term — is already spent, and what is left of it is what leaving costs. All three are time to a customer, so they are recorded as time and they add up.

The band describes the standard published terms rather than what a sales conversation might achieve, and a long one is a fact about the contract rather than a criticism of it — it is often what pays for a lower [entry price](/entry-price/). How the money leaves in the first place is [pricing features](/pricing/).

Infrastructure as a Service rents out the parts a data centre is made of — virtual machines, block and object storage, networks, load balancers — and leaves the assembly to the customer. Nothing physical is bought or maintained, and nothing above the hypervisor is managed either.

That is the whole trade: scale and flexibility against owning the operating system, the runtime and everything that keeps them patched. Records here answer "you do" to [who manages the operating system](/os-managed-by/), and the bill follows what is provisioned rather than a plan, which is why the [price ceiling](/entry-price/) recorded for a hyperscaler is where a serious deployment lands rather than the largest invoice it could issue.

IaaS is the ground floor of the cloud stack, with [PaaS](/category/paas/) above it and SaaS above that. The line blurs downwards rather than upwards: several providers here sell managed layers on infrastructure they own, and [whose hardware it runs on](/hardware/) is the field that tells an owner from a reseller.

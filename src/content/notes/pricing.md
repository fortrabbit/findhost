description: The parts of a price a reader is actually choosing between — a free tier or trial, paid upfront or in arrears, metered, prepaid, or with no minimum term.
---

The headline number is the least of it. What separates two hosts at the same monthly price is the shape of the arrangement: whether the money leaves before the month or after it, whether the bill is a figure or a consequence, and whether stopping is a decision or a wait. Each value here is read from a field on the record, so the detail stays where it can be checked.

**Paid upfront** means the plan is bought before it is used, and the worst case is known in advance: the bill cannot exceed the price. **Paid in arrears** means the meter runs first and the invoice follows, which is how a mistake at three in the morning becomes a number nobody budgeted for. **Metered usage** is the same idea sharpened — there is no plan, only consumption. **Prepaid credit** sits between them: money goes in first and drains, so the ceiling is whatever was deposited.

**No minimum term** means leaving costs nothing but the decision. How long the money keeps going out after that decision is its own field, [getting out](/exit/).

**Free tier** means something keeps running without payment, however small; **free trial** means free access that ends on a date or at a quota. Both are recorded and neither is rewarded. A free tier is a marketing instrument as much as a product, and it says nothing about what the paid plans cost, how the platform behaves under load, or whether the free thing resembles what would eventually be paid for. It is not part of the [entry price](/entry-price/) scale either, which measures what a provider charges rather than what it gives away. Where a trial decides anything, it is usually by settling the one question no comparison table can: whether the thing actually runs.

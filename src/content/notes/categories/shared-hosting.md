Shared hosting is the commodity end of the market: many tenants on one machine, a control panel, domains and email bundled in, and a price low enough to be an impulse purchase. The provider usually owns the data center and the metal, which also answers "owns the hardware" to [whose hardware it runs on](/metal/). Developer tooling is thin or absent, runtime versions run behind, and the advertised rate is usually introductory. Contracts run long and renew higher, which is why the dataset records a renewal multiple beside the [entry price band](/entry-price/). Neighbours are the other cost: a busy site next door is felt. It can host a brochure site, a hobby project or anything where the alternative is nothing at all. Where a deploy pipeline, a chosen runtime or [SSH](/shell/) matters, [VPS](/categories/vps/) and [PaaS](/categories/paas/) are the categories next door.

Shared hosting is the commodity end of the market: many accounts on one machine, a control panel over the top, domains and email bundled in, and a price low enough to be an impulse purchase. The provider usually owns the data centre and the metal, which is why this category holds most of the records that answer "owns the hardware" to [whose hardware it runs on](/metal/).

The price is paid elsewhere. Developer tooling is thin or absent, runtime versions run behind, and the advertised rate is usually introductory — contracts run long and renew higher, which is why the dataset records a renewal multiple beside the [entry price band](/entry-price/). Neighbours are the other cost: accounts share one machine's resources, so a busy site next door is felt.

It remains the right answer for a brochure site, a hobby project or anything where the alternative is nothing at all. Where a deploy pipeline, a chosen runtime or [SSH](/shell/) matters, [VPS](/categories/vps/) and [PaaS](/categories/paas/) are the categories next door.

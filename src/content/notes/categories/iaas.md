Infrastructure as a Service rents out the parts a data centre is made of — virtual machines, block and object storage, networks, load balancers — and leaves the assembly to the customer. Nothing physical is bought or maintained, and nothing above the hypervisor is managed either.

That is the whole trade: scale and flexibility against owning the operating system, the runtime and everything that keeps them patched. Records here answer "you" to [who manages the operating system](/operating-system/), and the bill follows what is provisioned rather than a plan, which is why the [price ceiling](/entry-price/) recorded for a hyperscaler is where a serious deployment lands rather than the largest invoice it could issue.

This is the shortest list on the site, and deliberately so: it means the global, usage-metered catalogue of hundreds of separately billed services. A European cloud selling instances by the hour from a handful of zones is a [VPS](/categories/vps/) with a longer menu, not a smaller version of this. A record may carry several categories, and most of the ones that used to sit here now carry two or three — but nothing here is also a managed platform, because the trade above is the whole definition.

A site is built inside the product — a visual editor, or lately a prompt — rather than written and deployed. Hosting, templates, updates and certificates come with it, which is what makes a site possible in an afternoon and what puts the runtime out of reach. There is no deploy step, so usually no [shell](/shell/), no runtime to choose and nothing to record under [software](/software/): the platform is the runtime. What it costs is portability. A site built in one of these leaves as content at best and as a screenshot at worst.

**Prompt-built is the fast-moving end of it.** Describe the site and the platform generates it, increasingly as real code — a framework, a repository, sometimes a deployment somebody else could take over. That last part is what will decide whether this stays a category of its own: a platform that hands over a git repository is a builder attached to hosting, and one that keeps the code is the same closed shape as a page builder with a better interface. The register records what the customer can actually take away — [deployment](/deployment/), [shell](/shell/), [runtimes](/runtimes/) — which is the question the marketing does not answer.

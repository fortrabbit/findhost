---
description: Which hosting providers publish something checkable about the energy they run on, and what the three kinds of green claim actually mean.
---

**Nearly every hosting provider says it is green. The claims are not the same claim.** In rising order of what they mean: **offsets** are bought after the fact, from somebody else, and are the cheapest thing to say. **Annual matching** buys as many renewable certificates over a year as the company consumed, which is what most "100% renewable" marketing means. **24/7 carbon-free** matches consumption hour by hour on the same grid, and only a handful of operators can claim it. Which of the three a provider claims is recorded on its record, in the energy claim field, because the distinction is real and the marketing depends on nobody drawing it.

The filter here asks a simpler question: does this provider publish anything at all? A host that names its claim and links the page it is made on has done something a host that says nothing has not. That is all a tick here means — the claim is theirs, not ours, and nothing on this site verifies it.

The [Green Web Foundation](https://www.thegreenwebfoundation.org/) does verify, against published evidence, and runs an open directory of the hosts that pass. Where a provider is in it, its record links there rather than restating the conclusion as ours. An absent listing is not a failing grade: their check works by IP address, so a host reselling capacity it does not own can be invisible to it either way.

Absent means unknown, never bad. A four-person host that publishes nothing may well run cleaner than a hyperscaler with a glossy report, and none of this measures the emissions of anything you would actually deploy.

---
title: What to look at when hosting WordPress
---

WordPress runs on almost anything with PHP, which is exactly why choosing a host for it is confusing: nearly every provider in this dataset can serve it, and the ones that market themselves at it are not automatically the better answer.

Three fields separate them. **Restrictions** is the sharpest — managed WordPress platforms routinely ban plugins, and a banned caching or backup plugin can be the reason a site does not work at all. **Backups** matters more here than on most stacks, because a WordPress site is a database plus an uploads directory that drift apart if only one is captured. And **who manages the OS**, because "managed WordPress" and "managed server" are different promises: the first updates your core and plugins, the second updates the kernel and leaves WordPress to you.

Worth knowing that a specialised platform buys convenience with flexibility. Records carrying `wordpress` under **specialised in** are built for it and usually restrict what else can run; records carrying it under **runs** simply document that it works.

---
title: How many people are behind a host
description: Hosting providers by the number of people who work there, in bands, from what each company says about itself.
lead: Three people, or three thousand.
---

Everything else in this register describes the product. This describes the company, and it is the question most likely to be asked out loud and least likely to be answered on a pricing page: how many people are behind this?

It matters in both directions, and neither one is better. A company of six answers support tickets with the people who wrote the platform, will still recognise a customer after four years, and can be gone in a quarter if two of them leave. A company of six hundred has a rota, a status page somebody owns, an escalation path, and a support reply written by someone who has never seen the code. Which of those a reader wants depends entirely on what they are putting on it.

Bands rather than a number, because the number is out of date the day it is recorded and nobody needs the precision. The source is the company's own account of itself — an about page, a team page, an imprint — so what is recorded is what a provider is willing to say in public. Where they say nothing, this stays unknown, which is the honest answer and will be the common one.

It is never scored. The [FindHost Signal](/signal/) ignores this field on purpose: a large provider is not thereby a good one, a small one is not thereby honest, and a register that turned company size into points would be picking a side in an argument the reader is better placed to settle.

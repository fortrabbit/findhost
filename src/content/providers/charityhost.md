CharityHost sells hosting from a WHMCS storefront in two shapes. The first, which it calls Optimal Web Hosting, is a Plesk account on shared hardware: a ladder of plans stepping up on CPU allowance, memory, PHP memory and NVMe space, each carrying unlimited sites and databases, Apache behind Nginx, PHP-FPM with selectable versions, Node.js under Phusion Passenger, MySQL and PostgreSQL, chrooted SSH, and the Plesk WordPress and Laravel toolkits beside Softaculous for one-click installs. The same plans are sold as reseller accounts with role-based users for a flat monthly surcharge. The second is KVM virtual servers under Virtualizor with root, custom ISO support and a choice of Linux or Windows, on Xeon and Ryzen hardware in Houston, Texas, beside HDD storage servers and an FTP storage product.

The discount is the positioning. Verified nonprofits, charities, NGOs and schools are given a recurring reduction, applied as account credit once proof of status has been sent through a support ticket after the order is placed, and the company says a portion of its proceeds goes to charitable causes.

## Worth knowing

Every invoice carries a [non-refundable administrative fee](https://charityhost.org/fees-schedule/), a percentage of the order plus a flat amount. The refund note printed on each plan page says refunds are not issued for discounted services, which is what a verified nonprofit is buying.

No legal entity or postal address appears on the site. The terms and the privacy policy are written for "CharityHost.org", place every dispute in the courts of Kansas, and route contact through the ticket system.

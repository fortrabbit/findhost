Namecheap is a domain registrar that sells hosting alongside registrations, which is the shape of a large part of the market: the domain brings the customer, the hosting is the second thing in the basket.

**The fields here are sparse for a reason that belongs on the record.** Every product and pricing page returns a refusal to an ordinary HTTP request, and answers only a browser. The knowledge base is the one part of the site that responds, which is why documentation is recorded and almost nothing else is. What Namecheap sells and what it charges is public to a reader and closed to a reader's tools, and the fields will stay empty until somebody fills them by hand.

That posture is itself worth knowing. A register like this one is assembled by reading what providers publish, and a provider that publishes only to browsers has decided which readers count.

Loudcloud was [founded in September 1999](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100813/000101287001500434/d10k405.txt) by Marc Andreessen, Ben Horowitz, Tim Howes, and In Sik Rhee. It sold servers, storage, networking, and the software on top, run for the customer as a service rather than assembled by them. At its core was Opsware, the company's own software for provisioning and configuring the servers.

The business ran into the end of the dot-com boom. It [went public in March 2001](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100813/000101287001500434/d10k405.txt) at $6 a share, and in June 2002 [sold the managed services business to EDS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100813/000095010902003392/d8k.htm) for $63.5 million in cash plus a license for the Opsware software. What remained renamed itself Opsware Inc. and sold the automation software on its own. [HP bought Opsware in 2007](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100813/000104746907005777/a2178943zex-99_1.htm) for $14.25 a share, about $1.6 billion. The same sequence, a hosting company selling the hosting and keeping the software it wrote to run it, recurs at [dotCloud](/dotcloud/) eleven years later.

Loudcloud was founded in September 1999 by Marc Andreessen, Ben Horowitz, Tim Howes and Sik Rhee, and sold what would now be called managed cloud infrastructure roughly a decade before the phrase existed: servers, storage, networking and the software on top, run for the customer as a monthly service rather than assembled by them. The part that made it work was internal — automation software called Opsware, written because provisioning and configuring machines by hand does not scale past a certain number of customers.

The business ran into the end of the dot-com boom. It raised $68 million before launch and another $120 million in mid-2000, went public in March 2001 at $6 a share after a far larger valuation was abandoned, and in June 2002 sold the managed services and hosting business to EDS for $63.5 million in cash plus licensing fees. What was left renamed itself Opsware and sold the automation software to everybody else, including to EDS, who now ran the hosting. HP bought Opsware in 2007.

It is recorded here because it is the ancestor of the category. Loudcloud's argument — that the interesting product is not the machine but the automation that operates it, and that a customer should rent the outcome — is the argument every managed platform in this register still makes. The company that made it first could not survive long enough to be it, and the software outlived the service.
